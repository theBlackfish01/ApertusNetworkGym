What this changes in the plan

  1. Teach the translator two preferences first. If those instructions were read right, the fine-tuned translator would be at 97.3 % on independent wording instead of 88.5 %, and at 100 % on our wording. This is the largest single gain available, and it needs only training sentences we can generate.
  2. Add other languages to the training wording. In German, French and Italian the fine-tuned translator's main mistake is a wrong or missing link (4.8 % of instructions).
  3. Test unseen instruction types properly before building on them. "Pass through a node" was obeyed 11 of 13 times (interval 58 to 96 %). The other types have one to five usable requests each.
  4. A hybrid needs a gate. Given a question or small talk, the decider rejected 9 of 35 requests. So the translator must separate "a routing instruction I have no rule for" from "not an instruction", and only the first may go to the decider.
  5. Keep the cross-check as a reported signal. It halves the error where both agree, at the price of many flags.
